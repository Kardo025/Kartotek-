How to group:
- A part is a meaningful task, roughly one 90-minute session, with about 3–6 topics. Coherence and complete coverage come first: never pad or drop material to reach a number. Put prerequisite material first.
- A topic is one objective the learner works through in one learning cycle. Never separate a procedure from its assumptions, a formula from its variables and units, or a claim from its exception.
- "kind" of a topic: "olgu" (a fact, term or formula to recall), "kavram" (a concept or cause-effect relation), "problem" (solving problems with a procedure), "yazma" (writing or arguing), "uygulama" (an applied skill).
- "objective": one sentence that starts "Without notes, …" and ends with an observable performance.

Each topic has one or more outcomes. An outcome has:
- "performance": an observable action ("Explain why A changes B when C", "Compute … with units", "Distinguish … from …"); never "understand" or "know".
- "object" (what it is about, a few words) and "conditions" (for example "without notes").
- "units" and "relations": the ids it covers.
- "criteria": 1–4 checkable statements, one sentence each, such as "States that …", "Computes … with the correct unit", "Names the condition under which …". An outcome that covers several units needs criteria that address each of them.

Coverage rule: every unit listed below and every relation must appear in at least one outcome. If a unit truly cannot be taught (unreadable, contradictory), put it in "unresolved_units" with a reason instead; use this rarely.
