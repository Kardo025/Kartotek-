# Priming rules v1.0 (Kartotek)

> **Retired (2026-10-01):** the user replaced this map-based priming with a simpler one (the part summary plus a Questions list; see STUDY-RULES.md). This file is kept for reference only; packages may still carry `priming` data, which the app ignores.

Source: the user's "Priming Kuralları v1.0" (2026-09-30), rendered in English. Purpose: before starting a new topic, build a temporary mental structure, a list of questions and a direction for learning.

In the app every part of a study runs: plan → [warm-up] → **priming** → learn → quiz → [second round] → teach → journal. The right-hand column says where each rule is applied.

## Core principle

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Priming prepares learning; it does not replace it. Active retrieval, questions, practice, feedback and spaced review must follow. | Priming is followed by the lesson, recall, quiz, teach and DSR scheduling. Priming writes nothing to DSR. |
| 2 | The goal is the big picture, not memorising details. Enough to know: the main parts, their likely relations, the most critical and most unclear areas, the questions to answer in detailed study. | The screen asks only for these: concepts, links, marks, questions, one-sentence big picture. |
| 3 | Do not seek certainty in the first round. The first map is a draft; "I think X affects Y" hypotheses are accepted and are later confirmed, corrected or deleted. | Copy says links are guesses; they are tagged after the lesson (Rules 22–23). |

## Time and timing

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Limit priming: short topic 3–5 min, medium part 5–10 min, wide unit or project 10–15 min. | `primingScope`: ≤2 objectives 3–5, 3–4 objectives 5–10, 5+ objectives 10–15 min. Priming is its own phase in the session budget. |
| 5 | If it runs long you are probably going into detail. In the first round do not read paragraphs line by line, memorise definitions, solve every example, take long tidy notes, or try to settle every link. | Stated on the screen; the phase timer warns when priming runs over its budget. Lesson text is not shown in priming. |
| 6 | Prime at the right time: before a new unit, a lecture or video, a new chapter, a broad exam review, a complex project or problem. | Priming opens each part, right before its lesson. |

## Scanning the scope

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | First find the learning goal: what should I be able to explain, what tasks, how is success measured, which earlier topics connect. | Section 1: the plan's goal, kinds of task, the quiz criterion, earlier parts. |
| 8 | Scan smartly: titles, sub-headings, outcomes, contents, bold terms, figures, graphs, tables, diagrams, the chapter summary, sample question types, slide headings. | Section 2: objective titles, block titles, bold terms, figures (thumbnails) and tables, generated from the package. |
| 9 | Take only the main ideas from several sources, in the order: syllabus or outcomes, the lecturer's slides or notes, textbook, reliable academic video, question bank. | Study content stays within the user's sources (STUDY-RULES.md). |
| 10 | Separate each source's function (slides: core structure; textbook: detail; video: process; article: evidence; question bank: expected application). | "Sources and what each is for" (`priming.sources`, or the slides by default). |

## Building relations

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Name at least three main concepts: "The three most important concepts may be ___, ___ and ___." | Section 3: at least 3 concepts (up to 8); scan terms can be added with one tap. |
| 12 | Link concepts with labels, not bare arrows: causes, affects, requires, limits, is compared with, is an example of, is part of, is a result of, comes before, comes after, is the opposite of, is similar to. | Section 4: concept · relation · concept rows with exactly these twelve labels; the map is drawn from them. |
| 13 | Build at least one process chain: starting condition → mechanism → intermediate result → final result. | Section 5: at least 3 steps. |
| 14 | Make at least one comparison: how are X and Y similar, how do they differ, when is X more suitable, what is the exception or limit. | Section 6: X, Y, the question it answers and a guess. |
| 15 | Call up prior knowledge explicitly: "What does this remind me of?" It may be wrong; the point is to make it visible. | Section 7 (required). The warm-up of earlier parts comes before priming. |

## Asking questions

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Mark unknowns, do not solve them now: ? I do not know · ! looks important · ↔ needs a link · ⚠ risk of confusion · → cause or process link. | Scan marks with these five symbols. |
| 17 | Write at least three targeted questions: definition, mechanism, why, comparison, condition, application, limit. | Section 8: at least 3 questions, each with its kind and a starter. |
| 18 | Make them open-ended and testable. Weak: "Photosynthesis?" Strong: "Why does the rate of photosynthesis not always rise with light intensity?" | A question counts only with 5+ words, a question mark and no "…" left; a hint says what is missing. |
| 19 | Turn the questions into targets: for each, where will I look for which evidence or explanation? | Each question names an objective (or "later"); in the lesson the questions aimed at an objective are shown above its text. |

## Metacognitive control

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Check yourself at the end: big picture in one sentence, at least three concepts, at least two relations, at least three questions. | Section 9: live self-check; the lesson starts only when it is complete (plus the chain, comparison and prior knowledge). |
| 21 | Do not take the feeling of comfort as evidence: familiar is not learned. Evidence is explaining without notes, solving correctly, applying to a new example, correcting errors, recalling later. | A "Watch out" note at the end of priming; recall, quiz and DSR measure the evidence. |
| 22 | Always update the first map after detailed learning: delete a wrong link, add a missing concept, or change a relation label (at least one). | Journal: "Check and update your priming map" requires at least one change. |
| 23 | Tag every hypothesis: ✓ confirmed · ~ partly right · X wrong or misleading · ? no evidence yet. | Journal: every original link must be tagged; the updated map shows the tags in colour. |
| 24 | Grow the map without over-complicating it: add a detail only if it helps understand a core concept, relation, mechanism or application. | At most 8 concepts and 10 links. |

## Moving to active learning

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | After priming the first step is targeted learning, not retrieval: map → questions → detailed source → self-explanation → closed-book retrieval → fix errors → update the map → plan spaced review. | Priming → lesson (with the student's questions) → self-explanation → recall → quiz and error log → journal map check → DSR. |
| 26 | Ask "why" and "how" for every main concept in detailed learning. | Self-explanation prompts in the lesson; the priming box repeats it. |
| 27 | A map is not complete until tested: explain without notes, rebuild in another order, apply to a new example, justify links, tell similar concepts apart. | Recall, teach and the quiz test it; the journal compares it with the reference map. |
| 28 | In spaced review, redraw the map from a blank page, complete missing nodes, write the labels, then compare with the original. | Journal: the source-based reference map is shown only after the student has tagged their own map. (Redrawing during later spaced reviews is not built yet.) |

## Limits and quality

| # | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | Priming does not make up for missing prior knowledge. For a very unfamiliar topic learn the basic terms briefly first, then map, then examples and practice. | If the plan's prior-knowledge ratings average 1 or less, "Basic terms first" opens with the part's terms (`priming.terms`). |
| 30 | Use a ready concept map as starting support, not as the final product. | Low prior knowledge: an optional starter skeleton (reference concepts and unlabelled pairs). |
| 31 | Do not confuse a map's looks with learning. | Stated with the reference comparison. |
| 32 | Judge priming by its results at the end of each unit: faster understanding, better questions, better links, more visible errors, recall a week later. | Study reflection E5 asks the first four; the objective cards in Review show the fifth. |
